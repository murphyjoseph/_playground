# Domain Profile: News / Current Events / Claim & Media Verification

For "what happened," breaking events, viral claims, and verifying images/video/quotes. The dominant risks are laundered provenance and false corroboration.

## SIFT — the four moves (default reflex)

1. **Stop.** Before sharing a conclusion or digging in, note your emotional reaction — strong emotion is exactly what manipulated content optimizes for.
2. **Investigate the source.** Leave the page. Search the outlet/account itself: who runs it, funding, track record, what independent sources say about it.
3. **Find better coverage.** Instead of verifying the specific article, search the *claim*. If it's real and significant, wire services and multiple independent outlets will have it. If only one cluster of aligned sources carries it, that's diagnostic.
4. **Trace to the original.** Quotes, statistics, studies, and media are routinely stripped of context. Find the primary document, full video, or original dataset before treating the framed version as fact.

## Corroboration rules

- Count **independent origins**, not article counts. Fifty outlets rewriting one wire story or one viral tweet is *one* source (Woozle effect). Trace each account back to its origin before counting it as corroboration.
- Prefer primary documents (court filings, official statements, transcripts, datasets) over characterizations of them.
- Distinguish an outlet's *reporting* (its own sourcing) from its *aggregation* (rewriting others). Only original reporting counts toward corroboration.
- A biased or non-credible source can still post content that independently verifies — verify the content on its own terms (two-axis separation). Symmetrically, a reputable outlet can mis-report; reputation is a prior, not proof.
- For fast-breaking events, expect early reports to be partly wrong. Timestamp your assessment and lower confidence accordingly.

## Media verification (images/video)

When authenticity of media is the question:

- **Reverse image search** across engines (Google, Yandex, TinEye) — the most common fake is real media from a different time/place, and prior appearances expose it instantly.
- **Provenance chain:** find the earliest posting; identify the original poster; ask whether they could plausibly have been there.
- **Geolocation:** match visible features (buildings, signage, terrain, road markings) against satellite/street imagery.
- **Chronolocation:** shadows, weather records, seasonal cues, visible clocks.
- **Forensics/metadata:** EXIF where available; error-level analysis (e.g., FotoForensics) for manipulation — treating results as suggestive, not conclusive.

Report a verification verdict as a chain: what was checked, what matched, what couldn't be checked. "Consistent with" is not "confirmed."

## Output posture for this domain

Lead with what is confirmed by independent origins, then what is reported-but-single-source, then what is disputed or unverifiable — explicitly separated. For contested events with competing narratives, run the ACH procedure from `evaluation-tools.md` rather than adopting the most-covered narrative.
